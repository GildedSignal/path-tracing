## 1. What the Papers Actually Say (with Section and Page Numbers)

Across the three primary Frazzoli works—the 2001 MIT PhD thesis (*Robust Hybrid Control for Autonomous Vehicle Motion Planning*, Technical Report LIDS-TH-2496), the 2002 journal paper (*"Real-Time Motion Planning for Agile Autonomous Vehicles"*, *AIAA Journal of Guidance, Control, and Dynamics*, Vol. 25, No. 1, pp. 116–129), and the 2005 journal paper (*"Maneuver-Based Motion Planning for Nonlinear Systems With Symmetries"*, *IEEE Transactions on Robotics*, Vol. 21, No. 6, pp. 1077–1091)—and their cited precursor on funnel composition by Burridge, Rizzi, and Koditschek (*"Sequential Composition of Dynamically Dexterous Robot Behaviors"*, *IJRR*, Vol. 18, No. 6, pp. 534–555, 1999), the architecture is split across papers in a way that abstracts alone conceal:
- **Frazzoli, Dahleh, Feron (2005)** treats **open-loop, obstacle-free steering** on a Lie group symmetry manifold via a regular language of trims and maneuvers, reducing fixed-sequence steering to algebraic/LP kinematic inversion in continuous coasting times.
- **Frazzoli (2001, Chapters 3–4)** and **Burridge, Rizzi, Koditschek (1999, Section 3)** treat **off-trim states and disturbances** via closed-loop feedback controllers whose **invariant tracking error tubes / funnels** satisfy set inclusion ($\text{Target}(\Phi_1) \subseteq \text{Domain}(\Phi_2)$).
- **Frazzoli, Dahleh, Feron (2002, Sections III–IV)** treats **obstacles** (which break global symmetry) by solving the **obstacle-free** Bellman value function $J^*(q, g)$ offline on the Maneuver Automaton and using it online solely as a distance metric/heuristic inside a **Rapidly-Exploring Random Tree (RRT)**.

### 1.1 Symmetries, Motion Primitives, and Trim Trajectories (Frazzoli et al. 2005, Sections II–III, pp. 1078–1081; 2001 Thesis, Chapter 2; 2002 JGCD, Sections II–III, pp. 117–120)

1. **System Class and Problem Formulation (2005, Section II, pp. 1078–1079):**
   - Considers a time-invariant nonlinear control system $\dot{x}(t) = f(x(t), u(t))$ on an $n$-dimensional smooth state manifold $\mathcal{M}$ (or $X$) with control inputs $u(t) \in \mathcal{U} \subset \mathbb{R}^m$.
   - Trajectories must stay within an operational envelope $F(x(t), u(t)) \le 0$ (Equation 2) and minimize an additive stage-cost functional $\mathcal{C} = \int_0^T g(x(t), u(t))\,dt$ (Equation 3; time-optimal control sets $g(x, u) \equiv 1$).
2. **Symmetry Group and Equivariance (2005, Section III-A, Definition 3.1, p. 1079):**
   - Let $(G, \circ)$ be a Lie group with Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$, acting on $\mathcal{M}$ via a smooth left action $\Gamma: G \times \mathcal{M} \to \mathcal{M}$ (also written $\Psi_g(x)$).
   - **Definition 3.1 (Symmetry Group):** The triple $(G, \mathcal{M}, \Gamma)$ is a symmetry group of $\dot{x} = f(x, u)$ if, for all $u(\cdot)$, $x_0 \in \mathcal{M}$, and $g \in G$, the flow $\varphi_u(t; x_0)$ is equivariant:
     $$\varphi_u(t; \Gamma(g, x_0)) = \Gamma(g, \varphi_u(t; x_0)) \quad \forall t \ge 0.$$
   - Both the operational envelope $F$ and incremental cost $g(x, u)$ are assumed **invariant** under $G$: $F(\Gamma(g, x), u) = F(x, u)$ and $g(\Gamma(g, x), u) = g(x, u)$ (Section III-B, pp. 1079–1080). **Crucial consequence:** Static workspace obstacles violate $F(\Gamma(g, x), u) = F(x, u)$ and are therefore excluded from the 2005 symmetry formulation.
   - In fiber-bundle coordinates (2001 Thesis, Ch. 2; 2002 JGCD, Section II, pp. 117–118), when $G$ acts freely and properly on $\mathcal{M}$, the state space decomposes locally as $\mathcal{M} \simeq G \times Y$, where $g \in G$ are the symmetry/group coordinates (e.g., position and heading in $SE(2)$ or $SE(3) / SO(2)$) and $y \in Y = \mathcal{M}/G$ are the internal "shape" or body-fixed velocity/attitude coordinates.
3. **Motion Primitives as Equivalence Classes (2005, Section III-C, Definition 3.2, p. 1080):**
   - **Definition 3.2 (Motion Primitive):** Two trajectories $\varphi_u(\cdot; x_0)$ and $\varphi_u(\cdot; y_0)$ defined on $[0, T]$ under the same control history $u(\cdot)$ are *equivalent* if $y_0 = \Gamma(g, x_0)$ for some $g \in G$. A **motion primitive** $\pi$ is an equivalence class of such trajectories under the left action $\Gamma$.
4. **Trim Primitives / Relative Equilibria (2005, Section III-C, Definition 3.3, p. 1080; Equation 4):**
   - **Definition 3.3 (Trim Primitive):** A trajectory $\varphi_u(\cdot; x_0)$ is a **trim primitive** (a control-theoretic *relative equilibrium*) if there exists a constant Lie algebra generator $\xi \in \mathfrak{g}$ and a constant control $\bar{u} \in \mathcal{U}$ such that for all $t \ge 0$:
     $$\varphi_{\bar{u}}(t; x_0) = \Gamma(\exp(\xi t), x_0), \qquad u(t) \equiv \bar{u}.$$
   - In bundle coordinates $(g, y) \in G \times Y$, a trim trajectory satisfies $\dot{y} = 0$ ($y(t) \equiv \bar{y}_q$ is a fixed point in shape/velocity space), $\dot{u} = 0$, and $\dot{g} = g \xi_q$ (so $g(t) = g(0)\exp(\xi_q t)$).
   - For terrestrial/aerial vehicles with symmetry group $G = \mathbb{R}^3 \rtimes SO(2)$ (3D translations plus yaw about the gravity axis; 2002 JGCD, p. 118; 2005 TRO, Section VII, p. 1086), trims are steady level flight at constant speed and climb rate ($\omega_z = 0$, pure translation) or steady coordinated helices/turns ($\omega_z = \text{const}$).

### 1.2 The Maneuver Automaton and How Maneuvers Compose (Frazzoli et al. 2005, Section III-E & Section IV, pp. 1080–1082; 2002 JGCD, Section III, pp. 119–121)

1. **Sequential Combinability / Concatenation (2005, Section III-E, p. 1080–1081):**
   - Two motion primitives $\pi_1 = (x_1, u_1)$ on $[0, T_1]$ and $\pi_2 = (x_2, u_2)$ on $[0, T_2]$ can be concatenated into $\pi_1 \pi_2$ on $[0, T_1 + T_2]$ if and only if there exists $g \in G$ such that the terminal state of $\pi_1$ equals the $g$-shifted initial state of $\pi_2$:
     $$x_1(T_1) = \Gamma(g, x_2(0)).$$
     In bundle coordinates $(g, y)$, this requires exact equality of the internal/shape state at the boundary: $y_1(T_1) = y_2(0)$, while the group coordinate $g$ absorbs the displacement via group multiplication:
     $$\pi_1\pi_2(t) = \begin{cases} (x_1(t), u_1(t)), & t \in [0, T_1] \\ (\Gamma(g, x_2(t - T_1)), u_2(t - T_1)), & t \in (T_1, T_1 + T_2]. \end{cases}$$
2. **Maneuvers vs. Trims as a Labeled Directed Graph (2005, Section IV, pp. 1081–1082):**
   - A finite set of **trim primitives** $Q_T$ (or $\mathcal{T}$) is chosen as the vertices (discrete states) of a finite-state machine called the **Maneuver Automaton (MA)**.
   - A finite set of **maneuvers** $Q_M$ (or $\mathcal{M}$) is chosen as the directed edges (transitions) between trim primitives. Each maneuver $\mu \in \mathcal{M}$ has a source trim $\tau_{\text{init}}(\mu) \in \mathcal{T}$, a target trim $\tau_{\text{fin}}(\mu) \in \mathcal{T}$, a fixed duration $T(\mu) > 0$, a fixed group displacement $g_\mu \in G$, and a fixed cost $c(\mu) > 0$.
   - As stated verbatim on p. 1081 (Section IV-A): *"The role of trim primitives has been, so far, just that of providing a convenient way of formally defining a special kind of motion primitives, namely, maneuvers, and providing them with a common interface for concatenation. However, trim primitives can be further exploited to enrich trajectories that can be represented using the MA language."*
3. **Motion Plans and Coasting Times (2005, Section IV-A, p. 1081):**
   - A **motion plan** on an MA is a pair:
     $$\sigma = (\omega, \tau) \in \mathcal{L}(\text{MA}) \times \bar{\mathbb{R}}_+^{k+1},$$
     where $\omega = (\mu_1, \mu_2, \dots, \mu_k)$ is a valid string of $k$ maneuvers in the regular language $\mathcal{L}(\text{MA})$ (passing through trims $\tau_0, \tau_1, \dots, \tau_k$ with $\tau_{j-1} = \text{source}(\mu_j)$ and $\tau_j = \text{target}(\mu_j)$), and $\tau = (t_0, t_1, \dots, t_k) \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^{k+1}$ is the vector of non-negative **coasting times** spent flowing along each trim primitive $\tau_i$.
   - The total group displacement of the motion plan $(\omega, \tau)$ starting from group configuration $g_0 \in G$ on trim $\tau_0$ is given by the exact group product (2005, Section IV, p. 1081–1082):
     $$g_f = g_0 \exp(\xi_{\tau_0} t_0) \, g_{\mu_1} \, \exp(\xi_{\tau_1} t_1) \, g_{\mu_2} \cdots g_{\mu_k} \, \exp(\xi_{\tau_k} t_k).$$
   - The total duration is $T(\sigma) = \sum_{j=1}^k T(\mu_j) + \sum_{i=0}^k t_i$, and by **Proposition 6.1** (p. 1084), the total cost of the motion plan is affine in the coasting times:
     $$\mathcal{C}(\sigma) = \sum_{j=1}^k c(\mu_j) + \sum_{i=0}^k c_t(\tau_i) t_i.$$

### 1.3 Completeness, Controllability, and Optimality Claims and Their Conditions (Frazzoli et al. 2005, Sections V–VI, pp. 1082–1085)

1. **Controllability of the Maneuver Automaton (2005, Section V, Theorems 5.1 & 5.2, pp. 1082–1084):**
   - **Definition (MA Controllability):** An MA is controllable if, for every pair of trim primitives $(\tau_a, \tau_b) \in \mathcal{T} \times \mathcal{T}$ and every pair of group elements $(g_a, g_b) \in G \times G$, there exists a motion plan $\sigma = (\omega, \tau)$ starting at $(g_a, \tau_a)$ and ending at $(g_b, \tau_b)$.
   - **Fixed-Point Motion Plans (p. 1082–1083):** A cycle in the MA graph starting and ending at the same trim $\tau_0$ whose net group displacement at particular reference coasting times is the identity $e \in G$.
   - **Theorem 5.1 & Theorem 5.2 (pp. 1082–1083):** The MA is controllable if and only if (i) the underlying directed graph $(\mathcal{T}, \mathcal{M})$ is strongly connected, and (ii) there exists a fixed-point motion plan whose reachable set in $G$ (under variations of the continuous coasting times $t_i \ge 0$) has a nonempty interior (checked via a Lie algebra rank condition on the Ad-mapped trim generators $\text{Ad}_{g_i}\xi_{\tau_i}$ spanning $\mathfrak{g}$).
   - **Crucial condition:** This proof relies on $t_i \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ being **continuous real variables** so that the Jacobian of the endpoint map $\Phi(\omega, \tau)$ with respect to $(t_0, \dots, t_k)$ has full rank $\dim(G)$ and the inverse function theorem gives an open ball in $G$.
2. **Optimality Claims and Suboptimality Caveat (2005, Section VI, pp. 1084–1085):**
   - **Explicit Suboptimality Caveat (p. 1084, verbatim):** *"On the other hand, restricting trajectories to combinations of a finite number of motion primitives results in the addition of additional constraints: hence, the solutions computed according to the proposed method will not, in general, be optimal."* "Optimal" in Section VI means optimal **among trajectories expressible in $\mathcal{L}(\text{MA})$** (Program 13), not optimal over all measurable controls $u(\cdot)$ of the underlying system.
   - **Program (13) and Remark 6.2 (p. 1084):** Minimizes the affine cost $\mathcal{C}(\omega, \tau) = \sum_{j=1}^k c(\mu_j) + \sum_{i=0}^k c_t(\tau_i) t_i$ over strings $\omega \in \mathcal{L}(\text{MA})$ and coasting times $t_i \ge 0$ subject to the boundary condition $g_f = \Phi(\omega, \tau, g_0)$.
   - **Theorem 6.3 (Existence and Finite Search, p. 1084–1085):** If the MA is controllable and every maneuver has strictly positive cost $c(\mu_j) \ge c_{\min} > 0$ (and $c_t(\tau_i) \ge 0$), then an optimal solution to Program (13) exists, has finite string length $k \le \mathcal{C}_{\text{ub}} / c_{\min}$, and can be found in finite time because only finitely many maneuver strings $\omega \in \mathcal{L}(\text{MA})$ have $\sum c(\mu_j) \le \mathcal{C}_{\text{ub}}$.
   - **Hierarchical Decomposition into Kinematic Inversion (p. 1085, verbatim):** *"The structure of (13) lends itself to a hierarchical decomposition of the search for the optimal motion plan into a combinatorial problem... and a smooth, generally nonconvex optimization problem, i.e., the determination of the optimal coasting times..."* For a fixed string $\omega$, finding $t_i \ge 0$ reduces to a **kinematic inversion problem** on the symmetry group $G$.
   - **Proposition 6.4 (Polynomial Program, p. 1085):** When the trim generators $\xi_{\tau_i}$ in $\omega$ generate a nilpotent subalgebra (or have zero angular rate after finitely many brackets), $\exp(\xi_{\tau_i} t_i)$ is polynomial in $t_i$, so Program (13) for fixed $\omega$ is a **polynomial program** in $t_i \ge 0$.
   - **Proposition 6.5 (Linear Program, p. 1085):** When the trim primitives in $\omega$ have **commuting generators** ($[\xi_{\tau_i}, \xi_{\tau_l}] = 0$—specifically, pure translational trims with zero angular velocity $\omega = 0$, where orientation changes occur only during the fixed maneuvers $\mu_j$), the endpoint constraint is **linear** in the coasting times $t_i$:
     $$p_f - p_0 = \sum_{j=1}^k \Delta p(\mu_j) + \sum_{i=0}^k v(\tau_i) t_i, \qquad t_i \ge 0,$$
     and since the cost $\sum c(\mu_j) + \sum c_t(\tau_i) t_i$ is affine in $t_i$, Program (13) for a fixed sequence $\omega$ reduces to a **Linear Program (LP)** in $t_i \ge 0$!

### 1.4 How They Handle Off-Trim States and Obstacles (Frazzoli 2001 Thesis, Chapters 3–4; Frazzoli et al. 2002 JGCD, Sections IV–V; Burridge, Rizzi, Koditschek 1999, Section 3)

1. **In Frazzoli et al. (2005):**
   - The 2005 paper assumes the system **starts and ends on a trim primitive** ($x_0$ is in trim $\tau_0$, $x_f$ is in trim $\tau_k$), or requires a dedicated entry maneuver from a rest trim (e.g., hover). It does not model off-trim initial perturbations or obstacles.
2. **In Frazzoli (2001 Thesis, Chapters 3–4) and Frazzoli et al. (2002 JGCD, Section V, pp. 123–125):**
   - Defines the **Robust Hybrid Automaton (RHA)**: each primitive $q$ (trim or maneuver) is paired with an **invariant static state feedback controller** $\kappa_q$ (2001 Thesis, Definition 4.1: $\kappa(\Psi_h(x), \Psi_h(x_{\text{ref}}), \bar{u}) = \kappa(x, x_{\text{ref}}, \bar{u})$), an **invariant tracking error set (tube)** $E_q$ around the nominal trajectory ( typically an ellipsoid from a quadratic Control Lyapunov Function / Riccati equation), and a **recoverability set (funnel)** $\mathcal{R}_q \supseteq E_q$ of off-trim states that $\kappa_q$ drives into $E_q$ in finite time.
   - **Chaining / Consistency Condition (2001 Thesis, Ch. 3–4; matching Burridge, Rizzi, Koditschek 1999, Section 3 "Safe Sequential Composition", pp. 537–540):** Primitive $q_i$ can transition to $q_j$ if and only if $q_i$ **prepares** $q_j$, meaning the exit error tube (goal set) of $q_i$ lies entirely inside the entry recoverability funnel (domain of attraction) of $q_j$:
     $$\text{TargetBound}(q_i) \subseteq \mathcal{R}_{q_j}.$$
   - When the vehicle starts at an **off-trim initial state** $x_0$, it can execute any primitive $q$ whose recoverability funnel $\mathcal{R}_q$ contains $x_0$; the low-level tracking controller $\kappa_q$ contracts the error onto the trim trajectory without replanning.
3. **How Obstacles Are Actually Handled in Frazzoli et al. (2002 JGCD, Sections III–IV, pp. 120–125):**
   - In the 2002 paper, Frazzoli et al. compute a Bellman value function $J^*(q, g)$ on the Maneuver Automaton by dynamic programming:
     $$J^*(x) = \min_{m \in \Phi(x)} \{ g(x, m) + J^*(\phi_m(x)) \},$$
     **but ONLY for the obstacle-free system** (where symmetry reduces the table to relative coordinates $g_1^{-1} g_2$).
   - Once obstacles are present (static or moving), the symmetry group $G$ is broken, and the precomputed table $J^*$ **cannot** give the obstacle-avoiding trajectory. Instead, the 2002 paper runs an online **Rapidly-Exploring Random Tree (RRT)** that samples random milestone states $x_{\text{rand}}$, uses the obstacle-free $J^*(x_{\text{tree}}, x_{\text{rand}})$ as the "nearest-neighbor" pseudo-metric, and grows the tree by concatenating maneuvers and checking each primitive's swept tube against obstacles.

### 2.2 Five Places Where Frazzoli's Assumptions Break in Minecraft's Law

1. **Continuous Lie group $G = \mathbb{R}^3 \rtimes SO(2)$ vs. IEEE-754 Binary64 Binades and the 65,536 Sine Table:**
   - Frazzoli assumes exact continuous translation and rotation invariance: $\varphi_u(t; \Gamma(g, x_0)) = \Gamma(g, \varphi_u(t; x_0))$ for all $(x, z, \psi) \in SE(2)$.
   - In Mojang's 26.2 law, **rotation symmetry does not exist** (of the 8 dihedral frames, only the identity `FRAMES[0]` replays bit-for-bit, `sources.md:85`, `event-forms/report.md:74`), and yaw enters through a 65,536-entry `f32` lookup table (`math.rs:5-17`, half-bin error $4.79 \times 10^{-5}\text{ rad}$, missing a $10^6$-block target by up to $47.9\text{ blocks}$, `redteam-opus.md:25-29`).
   - Even pure translation symmetry $x \mapsto x + \Delta x$ **breaks across binary64 binades** ($|x| = 2^k$, where ULP spacing $U = 2^{e-52}$ doubles) and at block-grid boundaries (`gait-modulo`). Inside one binade, $p + d$ is an exact integer map on mantissa and parity (`spec.md:50-52`, `P2`), not a real vector-space addition $p + q \cdot d$.
2. **Continuous Coasting Times $t_i \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ vs. Integer Cycle Counts $q_i \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$ and Whole-Tick $T$:**
   - Frazzoli's controllability proof (Theorem 5.1/5.2) and LP reduction (Proposition 6.5) rely on $t_i \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ being continuous real numbers so that $\sum v_i t_i$ hits a point target $x_f$ exactly.
   - In our law, time is whole ticks and `count` is an integer $q_i \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$, while the target $B$ is a detector box (`Goal::touched`, `spec.md:53,56`). Thus Proposition 6.5's continuous LP becomes an **exact two-integer interval intersection problem** ($\text{lo} \le E + q_1 D_i + q_2 D_{i+1} \le \text{hi}$, solved in $O(1)$ per binade/event regime by Euclidean quotient / floor-sum interval arithmetic, `spec.md:56-58`, `P3`).
3. **Homogeneous Free Space vs. Piecewise-Constant "Crystal with Defects" (Terrain & Collisions):**
   - Frazzoli (2005) has **no terrain and no collisions**: the symmetry group $G$ holds globally everywhere, and the nodes of the Maneuver Automaton are *global velocity trims* (hover, cruise, turn), with no spatial position attached.
   - When Frazzoli (2002) introduces obstacles, it **abandons** the Maneuver Automaton as an exact graph planner and falls back to online RRT sampling using the obstacle-free value function as a heuristic.
   - Our unified plan (`plan.md:10-12,38-44`) solves the problem Frazzoli (2005) omitted and Frazzoli (2002) handed to RRT: **local symmetry per zone**. In our design, translation symmetry holds *inside a zone* (where the corridor query finds no exception in the worldtube), and breaks at **defects (joints)**—spatial faces (lips, landings, class boundaries). Thus our graph nodes in acting are **joints in space** (holding sets of arriving states), whereas Frazzoli's MA nodes are **trims in velocity space**.
4. **Smooth Connected Lyapunov Funnels vs. Non-Monotone, Disconnected Single-Ulp Plateaus:**
   - Frazzoli (2001, Ch. 3–4) and Burridge, Rizzi, Koditschek (1999, §3) model a controller's domain and target as a single connected ellipsoid/funnel around a nominal point, and require **strict funnel containment** $\text{Target}(A) \subseteq \text{Domain}(B)$ for $A$ to chain to $B$.
   - In Mojang's discrete collision/step-up/edge law, the map from entry offset $s$ along a face to exit state and tick cost $T$ is **piecewise-constant and non-monotone** (`glm-digest/report.md:19-25`, `trial-offset-monotone`: on a 1-block gap, $T$ flips $12 \to 14 \to 12$ across two single-ulp boundaries, and continuous exit fields sawtooth across breakpoints).
   - Furthermore, an upstream word $A$'s exit set at a joint almost never lies *entirely* inside a single downstream plateau of word $B$: instead, $A$'s exit set overlaps several plateaus of $B$ (with different $T$-to-go) and may miss others entirely!
5. **Unbounded Infinite-Horizon Trims vs. Resource/Lease Events Inside a Gait:**
   - In Frazzoli (2005), a trim can coast for any $t_i \in [0, \infty)$ without internal state changes.
   - In Minecraft, even on uniform flat stone, a gait crosses **binade boundaries** ($|x| = 2^k$), **chunk-loaded leases** (`loaded(p, t)`), and on Normal difficulty **food exhaustion thresholds** (`food_level == 6` after 330 cycles / 3,949 ticks / ~1,407 blocks, where `(phase, exhaustion, saturation)` does *not* recur beforehand, `glm-digest/report.md:11`). Our `spec.md:48` (U1 fold rule) rightly treats these as a merged list of known events along the word rather than breaking the word into per-block decisions.

### 2.3 What We Would Get Wrong by Copying Frazzoli Literally

1. **If we copied Burridge–Rizzi–Koditschek / Frazzoli (2001) funnel containment ($\text{ExitSet}(A) \subseteq \text{EntrySet}(B)$), noted in `learning.md:129`:**
   - Look at `learning.md:129`: *"Frazzoli, Dahleh, Feron 2005 (chain when one exit set lies inside the next entry set)"*. First, that rule is in Frazzoli (2001 Thesis, Ch. 3–4) and Burridge et al. (1999, §3), not Frazzoli (2005) (which uses exact boundary matching up to $G$). Second, **requiring $\text{ExitSet}(A) \subseteq \text{EntrySet}(B)$ in our backward pass would break U2 and kill parkour tracks**:
   - In `hardtrack` and `fences`, a word $A$ arriving at a face from an upstream set produces a range of landing offsets $s$. Only a *subset* (one or more plateaus) of that landing range can reach the next narrow jump $B$. If chaining required $A$'s whole exit set to lie inside $B$'s entry set, $A \to B$ would be rejected!
   - **What we must do instead (and what `learning.md:52,55` and `plan.md:40,73` intend):** Backward composition is **preimage pullback (intersection, then pullback)**, not forward-tube containment. Specifically, at joint $J_1$, given downstream set $S_2$ at joint $J_2$ and an edge word $w: J_1 \to J_2$, the pulled-back plateau set at $J_1$ is:
     $$S_1(w) = \{ s \in \text{Face}(J_1) \mid f_w(s) \in S_2 \}, \qquad T_{J_1}(s) = \min_{w} \big( \Delta T_w(s) + T_{J_2}(f_w(s)) \big).$$
     For disturbance robustness (the 1-tick input delay / width of `vocabulary.md:69-73`), containment applies **only** to the noise cone (e.g. a pearl's random landing cone must lie inside the landing set, `plan.md:53`), never to the deterministic offset interval $s$, which is narrowed backward by preimage intersection and chosen forward as a single point!
2. **If we copied Frazzoli's bipartite state-vs-edge split (Trims = Nodes, Maneuvers = Edges):**
   - We would re-introduce two kinds of words ("gaits" vs "transitions") and two code paths. Our unified formulation (`plan.md:37`, `vocabulary.md:57`: every word takes a `count`; a gait has large `count`, a jump has `count = 1`) is strictly simpler and more general than Frazzoli's MA because a single word object handles both steady cyclic transport and 1-shot transitions.
3. **If we copied Frazzoli (2005)'s requirement that the start state be on a trim (or settled to rest first):**
   - We would repeat the `D7` mistake (`design.md:53`: re-simulating settling from the start state, or using a fed/settled clone, which failed the plain-path judge, `spec.md:12,69`). Instead, our `P1` (`spec.md:46`, `P1-judge/last.md:4`) memos the **observed transient** directly from the bot's actual observed motor bits onto the gait cycle in 263 ticks without settling.
4. **If we copied Frazzoli (2002)'s obstacle handling (obstacle-free DP table + online RRT):**
   - We would violate `AGENTS.md` ("No new search code") and `learning.md:39` ("No best-first search over exact states in acting"). Our zone-and-joint decomposition (`plan.md:38-44`) keeps exact symmetry closed-forms *inside* each obstacle-free worldtube (zone) and builds the exact min-plus T-to-go over the defect joints, needing no RRT.

## 3. What U1 and U2 Should Take from Frazzoli, and What They Should Refuse (with Line References to `jobs/unified-plan/plan.md`)

### 3.1 Concrete Confirmations and Refinements for Step U1 ("The kernel: slices C and D, with `count` an argument of every word")

1. **CONFIRM `plan.md:33,108,195` and `spec.md:55-60` (Two-Yaw Lattice as the Discrete Integer Form of Frazzoli's Proposition 6.5):**
   - *Why:* Frazzoli (2005) Proposition 6.1 (p. 1084) and Proposition 6.5 (p. 1085) prove that when coasting trims have commuting translational generators ($[\xi_i, \xi_j] = 0$, zero yaw rate $\omega = 0$), the cost is affine in coasting times and the endpoint map is linear in coasting times, reducing fixed-sequence steering to a Linear Program in $(t_0, \dots, t_k) \ge 0$ where generic 2D displacement requires at most **two** translational trims.
   - *What U1 takes:* Because our yaw is quantized to the 65,536-entry LUT (`math.rs`) and our `count` is an integer $q_i \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$, Frazzoli's Proposition 6.5 becomes the exact **two-yaw integer lattice** of `plan.md:108` and `spec.md:55-58` (`P3`): bracket the bearing by the two adjacent LUT yaws $i, i+1$, include the memoised start transient $E_{\text{start}}$ (`P1`) and the yaw-switch transient $E_{\text{switch}}$ (which corresponds to Frazzoli's maneuver displacement $g_{\mu_1}$ between trim $\tau_1$ and trim $\tau_2$!), and solve $\text{lo} \le E + q_1 D_i + q_2 D_{i+1} \le \text{hi}$ in $O(1)$ per binade regime.
   - *Crucial detail Frazzoli highlights for `plan.md:108`:* In Frazzoli's concatenation equation $g_f = g_0 \exp(\xi_1 t_1) g_{\mu_{12}} \exp(\xi_2 t_2)$, switching from trim 1 (yaw $i$) to trim 2 (yaw $i+1$) is **not** an instantaneous velocity rotation—there is a transition maneuver $g_{\mu_{12}}$ (in our law, the yaw-change transient from the carried velocity state of yaw $i$ onto the limit cycle of yaw $i+1$). `spec.md:55-56` already requires this (*"Include both start transient and the yaw-change transient from the real carried state; do not rotate velocity instantly"*), whereas `plan.md:108` says only *"my transient from the store (263 ticks, a memo hit), then cycles of 4.276060366589263 blocks per 12 ticks... with two neighbouring yaws whose counts land me in B's box"*.
   - **Concrete change to `plan.md:108`:** Explicitly mention the **yaw-switch transient** between `yaw1` and `yaw2` (in addition to the initial 263-tick transient from the observed start), matching `spec.md:55-56`.
2. **CONFIRM `plan.md:26,33,108` and `spec.md:44-52` (Quotient by Verified Partial Symmetry Only: $x, z, t$ out of the Motor Key, $y$ and Binade Kept):**
   - *Why:* Frazzoli's fiber-bundle reduction $\mathcal{M} \simeq G \times Y$ only factors out coordinates under which the law is *strictly equivariant* (Definition 3.1).
   - *What U1 takes:* In Minecraft's law, horizontal translations $(x, z)$ are equivariant **only within a binary64 binade** (`P2`, `spec.md:50-52`), and vertical coordinate $y$ is read relative to block collision shapes (as `P1-judge/last.md:18-23` Finding 2 caught when `position.y` was dropped from `motor()`). Therefore, U1's generator store (`spec.md:42-46`, Slice C) must keep `y` (or $y$ relative to the support top, translated on apply) in the recurrence key and must split horizontal translation at binade events (`spec.md:48-52`, Slice D).
3. **REFUSE Frazzoli's Bipartite Automaton (Separate Trim Nodes and Maneuver Edges) in U1 (`plan.md:37`):**
   - Keep `plan.md:37` and `vocabulary.md:57` unchanged: **one `Word` type taking `(program, arguments: aim, count)`**, where `count = 1` is a maneuver/transition and `count = q` is a coasting trim. Do not create separate `Trim` and `Maneuver` structs in Slice C/D.

### 3.2 Concrete Confirmations and Changes for Step U2 ("Joints and sets: the set record, reach between faces, one worldtube check per arc")

1. **CHANGE `learning.md:129` and CLARIFY `plan.md:40,73,196` on Set Chaining (Preimage Pullback vs. Funnel Containment):**
   - *Line reference:* `learning.md:129` cites *"Frazzoli, Dahleh, Feron 2005 (chain when one exit set lies inside the next entry set)"*, and `plan.md:40` defines a **Set** at a joint as *"The arriving states from which a known sentence reaches the target, with its T... a short sorted array of bit patterns with a T and a next word on each."*
   - *What U2 must take and refuse:*
     - **Refuse forward exit-set containment ($\text{Exit}(A) \subseteq \text{Entry}(B)$) for deterministic state coordinates (such as landing offset $s$ or arriving velocity class).** If word $A$ lands across an interval $[s_{\min}, s_{\max}]$ on face $J_1$ and word $B$ only succeeds from a sub-interval $[s_a, s_b] \subset [s_{\min}, s_{\max}]$, $A$ and $B$ **do** chain on the intersection $[s_a, s_b]$, pulling that sub-interval back to the upstream joint $J_0$! Requiring $\text{Exit}(A) \subseteq \text{Entry}(B)$ is the main reason a forward-tube thinker falsely declares narrow chained jumps impossible (such as the stalled joint on `fences`, `plan.md:196`).
     - **Reserve containment ($\text{Cone}(w) \subseteq \text{Set}(J_2)$) strictly for non-deterministic disturbance cones** (such as an ender pearl's random landing cone, `plan.md:53`, or 1-tick input delay width when sealing narrow joints, `vocabulary.md:70-73`).
   - **Concrete clarification for U2's Set Record (`plan.md:40,196`):** A joint $J$'s set is an union of disjoint **plateaus** $P_m = [s_m^-, s_m^+]$ (closed float bit-intervals bounded by single-ulp flips, `glm-digest/report.md:21`), where each plateau records `(s_lo_bits, s_hi_bits, entry_motor_class, T_to_go, next_word, next_joint, premises, prices)`. When pulling a downstream set at $J_2$ backward along a candidate word $w$ from $J_1$, U2 intersects the image of $w$ from $J_1$ with each plateau of $J_2$, finds the single-ulp preimage boundaries on $J_1$ by float bisection + replay (`plan.md:40`), and merges overlapping plateaus at $J_1$ by pointwise `min` of $T$-to-go.
2. **CONFIRM `plan.md:31,40,42` on When a Joint's Set Needs Velocity/Shape State vs. Offset $s$ Alone:**
   - *Why:* In Frazzoli (2005), a maneuver can only start from a state whose internal velocity/shape variable $y \in Y$ matches the trim equilibrium $\bar{y}_q$. When two maneuvers happen closer together than the relaxation time to trim, the internal velocity $y$ has not settled and must be carried in the state.
   - *How this confirms `plan.md:31,42` and warns U2:* `plan.md:31` derives momentum's memory length as 263 ticks (~22 cycles on stone). Inside a **site** (joints closer than momentum's memory, `plan.md:42`, such as `hardtrack`, `fences`, `birchparkourmedium`), arriving words have **not** settled to a single gait velocity!
   - **Concrete requirement for U2's Set Record (`plan.md:40`, `plan.md:147`):** Look at `plan.md:40`: *"It is stored as the law's own comparisons: the yes-or-no class, plus plateaus of the one coordinate a held word leaves free (its offset along its line)."* Compare that with `plan.md:147`: *"a wave over faces × arriving-speed classes (about 4 each, an input to count: about 400 nodes)"*, and `learning.md:51`: *"fences and fencemaze at one joint the field claims and no exact state realises"*.
   - **Warning for the U2 Set Record Red-Team (`plan.md:40,196`):** The yes-or-no class (`vocabulary.md:31`: booleans only—on ground, collisions, in fluid, climbable, sprinting) **does not distinguish arriving horizontal speed** (e.g., landing on a 1-block fence or stone lip after a 1-block hop vs. a full sprint-jump vs. an ice run-up all have the exact same yes-or-no booleans!). If U2 keys a joint's set *only* by `(yes_or_no_class, offset_s_plateau)`, it will merge slow and fast arrivals that share the same booleans—violating `vocabulary.md:102` (*"merging them is R-brach's kill: states were merged too coarsely at speed"*) and repeating the `fences` stall where the backward set claims a takeoff offset $s$ is valid using a speed that the upstream word did not deliver!
   - **Concrete fix for U2 (`plan.md:40,196`):** Inside a site (where joints are closer than the 263-tick contraction horizon), a set's entry key at a joint must include the **arriving velocity/gait regime** (or the upstream word's exit velocity along the takeoff direction, quantized only by the downstream word's single-ulp feasibility breakpoints, never by a made-up grid; `plan.md:147` already budgets ~4 arriving-speed classes per face). Beyond the 263-tick horizon, dissipation contracts velocity to the gait's fixed point (`plan.md:31`), so the arriving state collapses to the gait ID and phase alone.
3. **CONFIRM `plan.md:210` (Switch-Count Bound Remains a Prediction to Test, Not a Premise):**
   - Frazzoli (2005) Theorem 6.3 bounds the number of switches in an optimal MA plan only by $\mathcal{C}_{\text{ub}} / c_{\min}$ (using strictly positive maneuver costs), not by Pontryagin's linear $(n - 1)$ switch bound. `plan.md:210` rightly treats the switch count as an empirical check, not a premise.
